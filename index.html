PART I · ACTIVISION CENTRAL TECH

Solo-building a global C++ network probe across every GCP region.

My first mandate at Activision was to quantify real-world mobile and broadband conditions worldwide ahead of an internal mobile push. Entirely solo, I wrote a C++ telemetry client and deployed probe VMs in every Google Cloud region around the globe — measuring latency, jitter, packet loss, traceroute, path MTU, hop-by-hop MTU, packet bursting, and raw bandwidth across wired, Wi-Fi, and cellular links.

Built first on Windows with an external NMEA GPS puck and rapidly ported to Android with native GPS, I was proactive enough to take two laptops on a personal road trip from Los Angeles to San Francisco — one tethered to personal Verizon, one to corporate AT&T. The telemetry caught a textbook carrier routing divergence mid-drive: Verizon handed off cleanly to its Bay Area gateway halfway north, while AT&T stayed pinned to the Los Angeles gateway the entire corridor.

CHAPTER 03 DECEMBER 2017 — NOVEMBER 2020 INDEPENDENT ENGINEERING

No curriculum.
Just compilers, models, and grit.

Three relentless years teaching myself systems programming, native iOS engineering, full-stack web architecture, and classical machine learning from first principles.

Leaving university meant trading a syllabus for pure output. I dove headfirst into modern C++ and Objective-C, shipped an iOS app from scratch, sharpened my web engineering stack, and worked through Andrew Ng's original Coursera Machine Learning course down to the linear algebra.

"I applied to Comma.ai on their open dashcam speed-estimation challenge and failed — training computer-vision models without an NVIDIA GPU taught me more about hardware bottlenecks and persistence than any classroom could."

Every failed application sharpened the next build — from an OpenGL 3D game engine in C to a Counterfactual Regret Minimization blackjack bot — until Activision Central Tech called in late 2020.
