CHAPTER 02 NOV 30, 2020 — SEP 2024

Activision

Central Tech & Call of Duty: Warzone Mobile

PART I // CENTRAL TECH — GLOBAL NETWORK TELEMETRY

I broke into the industry on November 30, 2020, joining Activision's Central Tech division to support their internal mobile push. My first assignment was building a C++ network diagnostics suite from scratch—backed by a GCP VM in every region around the world.

Written entirely by me, it started as a Windows desktop application paired with an external NMEA GPS puck before I ported it to Android using native GPS. Across wired, Wi-Fi, and cellular links (including live Verizon vs. AT&T comparisons), it measured every layer of real-world link quality:

FIELD EXPERIMENT // INTERSTATE 5

The LA → SF Dual-Laptop Road Trip

To stress-test cellular handoffs in the wild, I took the project on vacation driving from Los Angeles to San Francisco—running two laptops side-by-side: one on personal Verizon, one on corporate AT&T.

VZW RTT: 24 ms AT&T RTT: 89 ms
PART II // CALL OF DUTY: WARZONE MOBILE

From Central Tech, I moved directly onto Call of Duty: Warzone Mobile. At the time, the game only supported full Activision, Battle.net, PSN, and Xbox Live logins—creating massive friction for mobile players who just wanted to drop in immediately.

I engineered Guest Login—allowing players to pick a username and jump straight into a match, directly lifting early mobile retention. Beyond onboarding, I shipped core work across the achievement engine, banning and anti-cheat fixes, and mobile match rejoin logic for flaky cellular connections.

CHAPTER 03 2018 — 2020

The Self-Taught Grind

C++ · Objective-C · iOS · Full-Stack Web · Machine Learning

With no safety net and no syllabus, I built my own curriculum. I went deep on C++ and Objective-C, shipped an iOS app from scratch, sharpened my web development craft, and worked through Andrew Ng's original Machine Learning course on Coursera.

CHAPTER 04 FALL 2017 — DEC 2017

UC San Diego

Computer Science Program · NCAA Water Polo

After high school, I enrolled in UCSD's Computer Science program while competing in NCAA Water Polo. One semester in, the gap was unmistakable: I knew I could learn, build, and grow faster on my own than the pace of a university curriculum.

CHAPTER 05 AUG 2013 — 2017

Crespi Carmelite High School

The Spark · Daily-Loan MacBook Airs · Water Polo

It started in August 2013 at Crespi Carmelite High School, attending on a heavy financial aid package. The school generously kept MacBook Airs available for daily loan—and every chance I got, I checked one out to write HTML, CSS, JavaScript, Java, Python, and C/C++.

I had appreciated technology my entire life, but those borrowed laptops were where appreciation turned into obsession. Between writing code and competing in water polo across all four years, the foundation for everything that followed was poured right here.
